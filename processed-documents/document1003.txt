**HOLIDAY NUMBER.**  
[For The Reader.]  
Christmas Eve.  

BY H. E. Z.  

MERRILY the bells were ringing,  
Joyfully the voices singing,  
Calling the old, and the young,  
"Peace on earth, good-will to men!"  

At the entrance gently swaying,  
We the branches interlacing;  
Into the church three, three,  
Pass the portal, through the light,  
Lined a flood of dazzling light.  

Through the streets and darkness streaming,  
On wings of prayer the firmament,  
O'er the world thronging, lowly,  
Sitting on the side and high,  
Listening to the solemn holy,  

Hear the bells that now are sending  
Echoes to the open portal,  
Of the Christ-child’s bright glory,  
For His name’s the name of story,  
This to us He has revealed,  
To serve and be His shield.  

Death may stoop to honor preference,  
The most wretched not despising;  
Saved them at a precious cost—  
(Here the figure nearly falls),  
For He came to save us all.  

Cephal, that flower, past the rising,  
From the glory and darkness lifting,  
Just to spread the trembled standings  
On which the floor was stained.  
Trying while me so tender grace,  
Every sorrow now seemed grace.  

Such is here past defining,  
Such is here of glory bright;  
"Noble Father, hast thou spoken,  
"God, my Father, Thou dost speak,  
"Take me, take me, O Heaven,  
"Take me for dear Jesus’ sake.  

"Little me home," the toddler, kneeling;  
The doors, "Humbly, praying,  
"Nobody but me, for I see,  
"No one ever slept till early."  

No one but the one Great Being,  
Eye too bent, His prayer’s granted—  
And the voices soft were chanted,  
And the world was expanded,  
"Peace on earth, good-will to men."
