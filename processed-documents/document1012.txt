In the field of musical instruments.

Remarkable as it may seem, there is a strange and, we might say, a mysterious bond between the various departments of music. The confusion that often exists in the minds of those who study music has its origin in the mistaken belief that these branches are entirely distinct one from another. The truth seems to be that between the different forms of expression, whether vocal or instrumental, there exists something that binds them together, and renders each subsidiary to the other in a manner that is difficult to define.

The same principles which are applied in the crafting of a song find their place in those who would play either the classical or modern instrumental compositions. We have all doubtless heard of the wonderful acquaintance which seems to exist between certain kinds of music; and while it may seem that the student has given his or her entire attention to a certain phase of musical art, yet one may safely say that behind all this singularity there lies a bond that ties together the music of every period and nationality.

We cannot have too much confidence in the workings of our intuitive faculties, which are the natural characteristics common to all people. We often hear musicians referring, with much delight, to the influence of certain performances upon their emotional states. After a very careful study, one can easily perceive the effect that this or that composition has upon the performer. 

Many years ago, an eminent critic, whose name has become identified with musical criticism, described some of the great American composers as "the great unknown." It is strange how many people have felt a mystery surrounding certain aspects of performance, and yet, it must be acknowledged that even though these aspects may seem complex and difficult to understand, their appeal is universal.

The "musical beholder," which appears in the attention of the audience, cannot be excluded from any performance that takes place, and yet the audience has a part in the performance that should not be overlooked. Many listeners feel the performance as music itself and take a sort of spiritual comfort in it without being aware of their own responses.

In this connection, the aspect of the concert appears to play a vital role. The concert hall is often a place where elementary emotions intertwine with the sublime experience. One can say that the composer, the publishers, and the performers all have their influences converging toward a single point: the audience.

This analysis leads us to the conclusion that in any musical performance, all elements are present and participate in the interplay of emotional expression and artistic beauty. The performer, whether in the form of a soloist, an ensemble, or an orchestral body, brings together a certain connection with the music being rendered, and brings to bear upon it a subjective application that can resonate deeply within those present. 

Without doubt, the continuous search for principles and precepts in our musical education does not end when we leave our teachers or conservatories. The journey continues and must embrace conscientious exploration, as we seek to understand the deeper meanings behind the music we play or listen to. 

In order to support this argument, we can put forward that Mr. Shaw, who is a good friend of ours, was trying to show the reader that there is indeed more than meets the ear. It has been said that “music speaks where words are unable to explain.”

Music, therefore, in its essence, transcends mere auditory experience, for it contains within itself the means to develop a deeper sense of being and understanding. 

One particularly remarkable feature of good playing is the ability to involve the listener’s emotions, and, while many know of the traditional importance attributed to the technical aspects of performance, there are still others who consider it equally important to experience the emotional journey that accompanies musical performance.

Thus, the relationship between music and the audience constitutes a sort of rich tapestry, woven together through the threads of emotion and technique. Even where we have not the means to fully articulate our thoughts, we feel the stirring impulses set in motion through the artist’s performance.
